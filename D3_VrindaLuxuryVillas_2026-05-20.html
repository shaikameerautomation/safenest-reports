Things to Confirm With Sales Team

Questions worth asking before you book

These are not warnings — they are things we found during due diligence that deserve a direct conversation with the developer before you sign anything.

11-Party JV Land Structure — Confirm Your Specific Plot's Title Chain
The land for this project was assembled from 9 individual landowners and 2 corporate entities, each of whom signed a separate registered Development Agreement cum General Power of Attorney (DAGPA — Document nos. 487/2022, 488/2022, 27080/2022 and others). All DAGPAs are government-registered and the 42-year Encumbrance Certificate is clean across the entire parcel. The practical concern is that with 11 separate counterparties, any one of them could raise a dispute about their specific portion of land — and each villa plot sits on a title chain that must be independently traced. A project-level clean EC does not guarantee that your specific plot number carries no historical claim requiring resolution. The absence of fire NOC documentation also means one clearance remains to be confirmed.
What to do: Engage an independent property lawyer to review the full DAGPA chain for your specific plot number. Ask the developer for the list of all 11 counterparties and a map showing which plots correspond to each DAGPA — this is a standard, reasonable request for a registered multi-party JV.
Developer Track Record — Prior OC Dates Not Independently Verified
Abinandana Infra Reality LLP has completed multiple standard apartment projects and one mid-rise, confirmed by our advisor Ameer through direct conversations. We were not able to independently verify the Occupancy Certificate dates for these prior completions on the TSRERA portal — which means we cannot confirm whether they were delivered on time or with delays. For a 99-villa project targeting possession in 19 months with JV execution complexity across 11 counterparties, the developer's history of on-time delivery is a more important data point than it would be for a smaller or simpler project. The midpoint score of 5/10 reflects the uncertainty, not a negative finding.
What to do: Ask the developer for RERA registration numbers for their completed projects. Visit tsrera.telangana.gov.in and search those numbers to check OC dates directly — a 5-minute check that tells you whether they delivered on time or not.
No Scheduled Bank Home Loan Approvals Confirmed in Documents
No home loan approval letters from scheduled banks — SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis, or others — appear in the documents reviewed for this report. Scheduled bank approval matters to buyers because it signals that the bank's legal and technical team has independently reviewed and cleared the project for lending — an additional due diligence layer that also opens up competitive home loan options. The Indian Overseas Bank construction loan (MoD) on the EC is the developer's own financing facility; it is not buyer home loan approval and does not extend to individual villa purchasers. Without scheduled bank approvals in place, your financing options may be limited at the time of booking.
What to do: Before booking, approach at least one scheduled bank — SBI, HDFC, or ICICI — with the project address and RERA number P01100010002 to check whether they will approve a home loan for this project. Ask the developer whether approvals are in process and when they expect them to be confirmed.
19-Month Possession Target — Tighter Than Typical for This Stage
The developer has confirmed December 2027 possession — approximately 19 months from today. Structure work is currently below 50% complete across 99 villas. SafeNest's physical consistency check flagged this as a Conditional Pass: a project at Structure WIP <50% typically implies 24–36 months to completion, making 19 months a tighter-than-standard schedule. The developer's verbal confirmation has been accepted by our advisor, and RERA provides a degree of protection — if possession is delayed, RERA entitles buyers to interest compensation. The combination of JV execution complexity and early construction stage makes this timeline worth confirming in writing before you sign anything.
What to do: Ask the developer for a written milestone schedule showing specific completion targets for structural work, finishing, and OC application, tied to the December 2027 date. Cross-reference with the RERA project page at tsrera.telangana.gov.in under P01100010002.

Common Questions

Questions buyers usually ask

Is the developer reliable for a project of this complexity?
Abinandana Infra Reality LLP has completed multiple standard apartment projects and one mid-rise, confirmed through our advisor's direct conversations. The limitation is that OC dates for these completions could not be independently verified on the TSRERA portal — meaning we cannot score their on-time delivery track record at full confidence. For a 99-villa, 11-party JV project with a 19-month target, execution complexity is meaningfully higher than a single-entity apartment. The practical check: ask the developer for RERA numbers for completed projects, then verify OC dates yourself at tsrera.telangana.gov.in in under 5 minutes.
What happens if they delay possession beyond December 2027?
Under RERA rules, if the developer fails to deliver by the RERA-registered date, you are entitled to compensation at the SBI MCLR rate on all amounts paid — currently around 8–9% per annum. You can also choose to exit the project and receive a full refund with interest, or continue and receive delay interest from the developer while waiting. The 3-account RERA escrow at Indian Overseas Bank means construction funds are protected from diversion — the escrow mechanism ensures buyer payments are ring-fenced for construction only. A delay costs the builder money, not just you time.
Is ₹9,999 per sft a fair price for what you get here?
For the Kollur micro-market, ₹9,999/sft for a 3-storey private villa with Jaguar fittings, hydraulic lift provision, 2 covered parking bays, and 283 sft of shared clubhouse amenity is well-positioned. Premium apartment projects in the same belt price at ₹7,000–9,500/sft — but those are apartments, not private villas on ~4,672 sft of land with 2 covered parking slots. The nearest comparable villa communities in the belt typically price at ₹10,500–13,000/sft. The product justifies the price if you are comparing it to other villas of similar scale and amenity — less so if you are treating it as an apartment-equivalent investment.
What is the maintenance cost after moving in?
For a 99-unit villa community with a full-service clubhouse — restaurant, spa, squash, indoor badminton, supermarket — maintenance costs will be higher than a typical apartment project. The shared amenity is large relative to the number of contributing families, and individual villas carry upkeep costs that apartments distribute across more units. A reasonable estimate for comparable premium villa communities in Hyderabad is ₹8,000–15,000 per month, depending on the final maintenance structure agreed at handover. Confirm the corpus amount and per-villa monthly charge with the developer before signing the Agreement of Sale.
Which banks have approved home loans for this project?
As of the date of this report, no scheduled bank home loan approvals — SBI, HDFC, ICICI, or Axis — are confirmed in the documents reviewed. The Indian Overseas Bank MoD on the EC is the developer's construction loan; it does not translate to buyer home loan approval. Before booking, check your loan eligibility directly with at least one major bank using the project address and RERA number P01100010002. If the bank cannot locate the project in their approved list, ask the developer when approvals are expected and consider making bank approval a stated condition of your booking agreement.
How do I track construction progress between now and December 2027?
The most reliable method is the TSRERA portal — developers are required to upload quarterly construction updates including photographs and progress reports. Search for P01100010002 at tsrera.telangana.gov.in and check the Updates section regularly. Supplement this with planned site visits at each CLP milestone: when you are asked to make a construction-linked payment, visiting the site to verify that the milestone genuinely matches what you are being asked to pay for is both your right under RERA and a practical protection in a project with a tighter-than-typical schedule.

Where we found differences between the brochure and official records

In preparing this report, we cross-checked the developer's brochure and price sheet against TSRERA filings, HMDA approval records, Encumbrance Certificates, and independent legal opinions. Where we found differences on facts that may matter to your decision, we list them here so you can ask the sales team directly.

Project Name — Brochure vs. RERA-Registered Name
The developer's brochure and all marketing materials refer to this project as "Vrinda Luxury Villas." RERA filings and legal documents — including the DAGPA and Encumbrance Certificate — register the project under "Vrinda Villas Lorena Phase-II." Both names refer to the same project. The brochure name is used throughout this report per Ameer's instruction. When verifying on the RERA portal, search for RERA ID P01100010002 or "Vrinda Villas Lorena Phase-II" — not the brochure name.
What to ask the sales team: Confirm that RERA registration P01100010002 under "Vrinda Villas Lorena Phase-II" covers all 99 Phase 2 villas, and that your Agreement of Sale will reference the correct RERA-registered project name.
Unit Count — RERA Transcript (60) vs. Physical Phase 2 Count (99)
The RERA transcript for P01100010002 states 60 proposed building units for Phase 2. The physical villa count for Phase 2 is 99 (out of 198 total villas across both phases). All formula fields in this report — density, parking ratio, amenity ratio, and land per unit — have been computed on the 99-villa physical count per Ameer's instruction. The RERA-registered unit count of 60 is retained in the RERA field; buyers should confirm with the developer which specific villa numbers fall under the RERA registration.
What to ask the sales team: Confirm that your specific villa plot number is included within the RERA-registered Phase 2 units under P01100010002, and request a copy of the RERA site plan showing all 99 villas.
Clubhouse Area — Total Project vs. Phase 2 Share
The total clubhouse built-up area across both phases is 56,053 sft for all 198 villas. The Phase 2 proportionate share — used throughout this report for the amenity-per-unit calculation — is 28,027 sft (56,053 × 99 ÷ 198). The amenity per villa figure of 283 sft reflects this proportionate share. Buyers should confirm whether Phase 2 residents have full access to the entire 56,053 sft clubhouse, or only to the Phase 2 portion, as this would affect the effective amenity per family.
What to ask the sales team: Confirm whether Phase 2 villa owners will have full access to the entire clubhouse across both phases, or only to the Phase 2 allocated section. Get this in writing in the Agreement of Sale.
Open Space — HMDA Phase 2 Figure (10.03%) vs. Project-Wide Figure (40%)
The HMDA layout plan records an open space ratio of 10.03% specifically for the Phase 2 land parcel. The project-wide open space ratio across the full 198-villa development is 40%, confirmed by Ameer. The 40% figure has been used for the Luxury Index computation per Ameer's instruction. Buyers should be aware that the green space visible and accessible within Phase 2 specifically may reflect the lower HMDA Phase 2 figure rather than the project-wide total.
What to ask the sales team: Request the HMDA-approved site plan for Phase 2 showing the open space allocation, and confirm what green and open areas are physically located within the Phase 2 boundary.
Villa Lift — Provision Only, Not a Free Installed Lift
Initial documents reviewed indicated "hydraulic lift in every villa" as a standard inclusion. The corrected position is that the villa structure includes the hydraulic lift shaft and provision — the structural space, reinforcement, and mechanical prep — but the lift unit itself is owner-procured and installed at the buyer's own cost. This is a meaningful distinction: the provision is standard and included in the base price, but buyers should budget separately for the lift unit and installation (typically ₹3–6 lakh for a residential hydraulic lift).
What to ask the sales team: Confirm exactly what is included in the lift provision — shaft dimensions, pit depth, load specifications — and get a written specification sheet so you can obtain accurate lift unit quotes before your Agreement of Sale.
Active Mortgages — Institutional and Expected, but Discharge Timeline Matters
The Encumbrance Certificate notes two institutional entries: an HMDA regulatory mortgage covering 5 villas (plots 70–74, Deed 445/2024) and an Indian Overseas Bank Memorandum of Deposit of Title Deeds — the developer's construction loan facility. Both are standard for a HMDA-regulated development with third-party construction financing and do not constitute adverse encumbrances. However, the IOB MoD means the original title documents are held by the bank as loan security until the construction loan is fully repaid — and individual villa registrations cannot proceed until the MoD is discharged.
What to ask the sales team: Request the outstanding construction loan amount under the IOB MoD and ask for the discharge timeline — specifically, whether the MoD discharge will happen before or simultaneously with individual villa registrations for Phase 2 buyers.
